Background

Connecting mathematics, computation, and engineering.

My work focuses on developing and applying numerical methods to complex engineering problems—from governing equations and numerical formulation through implementation, verification, validation, and analysis.

For my M.S. research, I developed a finite-volume solver for the compressible Euler equations to investigate a hybrid Roe-WENO numerical method. I built the solver from scratch and implemented it independently in MATLAB, C++, Python, and Julia.

My broader engineering experience includes computational analysis of thermal-fluid and high-speed-flow systems, including CFD, heat transfer, aerodynamics, and multidisciplinary engineering problems.

I’m particularly interested in problems where numerical behavior, physical modeling, and engineering interpretation must be considered together.

Selected research

Hybrid Roe-WENO Euler Solver

A finite-volume solver for the compressible Euler equations, developed for my M.S. research to investigate the accuracy and stability of a hybrid Roe-WENO numerical method.

The research examined introducing central differencing into the average-flux contribution of the Roe numerical flux while retaining WENO reconstruction for shock capturing.

The implementations model shock waves, contact discontinuities, and expansion waves, with comparisons against established benchmark solutions.

Approach

A solution is useful when its behavior is understood.

I emphasize comparison against analytical or established reference solutions, investigation of numerical stability, and quantitative evaluation of discretization error and solution accuracy.

My current interests include high-order methods for conservation laws, Krylov subspace methods, sparse numerical linear algebra, distributed-memory parallelism, GPU computing, and performance analysis and optimization.
